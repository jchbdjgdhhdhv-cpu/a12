Chapter II

Hardware Care

Buckles, rings and clasps take more wear than any other part of a bag. A few minutes of care keeps them bright and working.

I

Chapter II · part I

Wipe little and often

After use, wipe hardware with a soft, dry microfibre cloth to remove fingerprints and moisture. This alone prevents most dullness and tarnish.

II

Chapter II · part II

Protect the leather first

When cleaning metal, slip a piece of card or folded paper behind the buckle so polish never touches the leather. Many metal polishes will stain or dry it out.

III

Chapter II · part III

Clean by metal

  • Solid brass: a tiny amount of brass polish on a cloth, then buff. Or let it darken to a warm patina.
  • Nickel and steel: a dry cloth; a barely damp one for marks.
  • Plated: dry cloth only. Polish can rub through the plating.
  • Coated or black: dry cloth; avoid anything abrasive.
IV

Chapter II · part IV

Keep it dry

Moisture causes tarnish and can leave green marks on leather. Store bags in a breathable dust bag, never sealed plastic, with a sachet of silica gel in damp climates.

V

Chapter II · part V

Repair early

A loose rivet, a bent prong or fraying stitching around a buckle is easy and inexpensive to fix early, and expensive to fix late. A good leather repairer can replace hardware entirely.
