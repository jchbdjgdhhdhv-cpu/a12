Chapter I

Choosing a Buckle Bag

A buckle bag is only as good as its leather, its hardware and the stitching that holds them together. Here is what to look for.

I

Chapter I · part I

Start with the leather

Full-grain leather keeps its natural surface and develops a patina over time. Top-grain is lightly corrected and more uniform. Avoid bags where the leather feels plasticky or cracks when you bend it.

II

Chapter I · part II

Feel the hardware

Good buckles feel heavy for their size, with smooth edges and no rough casting marks. Solid brass and stainless steel last for decades; plated metals look beautiful but can wear at contact points.

III

Chapter I · part III

Check how it is fixed

Look where the buckle meets the bag. It should be held by a stitched leather tab (the chape), often with rivets. Glue alone will fail. Even, tight stitching with no loose threads is a sign of care.

IV

Chapter I · part IV

Try the fastening

  • Can you open it with one hand?
  • Does the strap slide easily through the keeper?
  • Are the holes reinforced and cleanly punched?
  • Is there a hidden magnet or clip for quick access?
V

Chapter I · part V

Think about daily use

Traditional tongue buckles look beautiful but are slower to open; turn-locks and push-locks are quicker. If you reach into your bag often, choose a bag with a hidden magnetic catch behind decorative buckles.
